- question ≥ 10 characters; explanation ≥ 10 characters.
- Plain UTF-8 text only. Mathematical symbols and inline equations (μ, σ, π, β₀, χ², √, ≤,
  →, etc.) are welcome. No HTML, no Markdown.
- LaTeX is permitted for maths that Unicode cannot set cleanly (fractions, integrals,
  sums, matrices): wrap inline maths in \(…\) and display maths in \[…\], NOT $…$
  ($ is a literal dollar sign and must stay one — e.g. "paid $500"). In JSON every
  backslash MUST be doubled: write "\\(\\frac{2}{3}\\)", never "\(\frac{2}{3}\)".
  Unicode symbols remain fine; use LaTeX only where it reads better. Both render correctly.

Taxonomy rules (prefer the list; extend only when needed)
- Prefer the subject ids and subtopics in the taxonomy below.
- If the material genuinely fits no listed subject or subtopic, you MAY introduce a new one.
  Follow the conventions above: subject id lowercase_snake_case, subtopic Title Case.

Quality rules
- One defensibly correct answer. Exactly one option is correct to a knowledgeable expert.
  If a topic can't be made unambiguous, choose a different subtopic.
- Distractors from real misconceptions. Each wrong option reflects a plausible mistake a
  learner might actually make — never random nonsense.
- The answer must not be guessable from surface form. Keep all options in a similar length
  band and the same grammatical form. Do NOT make the correct option the longest, the most
  qualified, or the most "textbook-complete" one. Options are shuffled on ingestion, so
  position carries no information — never reference an option by position or letter, in the
  question or the explanation.
- Use true/false only for genuinely binary facts, not as a lazy default. Vary your formats.
- No meta options. Never "All of the above", "None of the above", "Both A and B", etc.
- Explanations teach the learner. Define the concept and show the method or reasoning that
  reaches the answer. Do not mention distractors, "common mistakes", option positions, or
  anything about how the question was designed.

Difficulty calibration
- easy — direct recall or one-step recognition (one fact, definition, or method name).
- medium — apply a concept to a scenario, compute a result, or distinguish closely related
  concepts.
- hard — edge cases, multi-step reasoning, subtle behavior, or separating a correct idea
  from a common misconception.

Variety within the batch
- Vary the subtopic, the question style (definitional / applied / comparative / true-false),

- No duplicates or near-duplicates of one another, and no proprietary or trademarked content.
